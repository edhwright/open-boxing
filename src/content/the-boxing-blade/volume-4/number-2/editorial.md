# Editorial

Supervision of boxing in states where the commission plan is in effect was presumably placed under these officials for the purpose of keeping the sport within the law.

In other words, the commissions are empowered to regulate boxing and to see that it is conducted according to law.

But there have been many instances where commissions have overstepped their legal authority and have assumed powers that do not legally belong to them.

Boxers and managers are barred indiscriminately for little or no reason except to satisfy the egotism of some officious officials who crave to show and exercise their authority. It makes no difference whether these officials are fitted to pass judgment on the acts or alleged offenses of the individuals who incur their disfavor or displeasure.

Granting that some boxing commissioners are capable of filling their office, it has been conclusively proved that many of them don't know what it's all about.

It may truthfully be said that comparatively few of these officials are competent to judge the merits of anything connected with boxing. How can officials be expected to rule wisely when they are political appointees who don't know any more about boxing than an Eskimo knows about sunstroke, and who think that an uppercut is something you get in a butcher shop.

This is no fantastic exaggeration in an attempt to be funny. It is a fact that not long ago the chairman of a boxing commission in a state not a million miles from here, proposed to a promoter that he match a well known middleweight and a prominent bantamweight. Any newsboy would have known better.

As to the exceeding of their authority by some boxing commissions, an example is had in the case of Jack Dempsey. Here we have the champion of the world barred from fighting any except the opponents picked for him by the state commissions. It is a sure-thing 1000 to 1 bet that the general public, in whose interest they pretend to act, know more about the relative merits of Dempsey's contenders and their chances with the champion than does 90 per cent of the boxing commissions that presume to think and decide for them.

There are other cases too numerous to mention here.

It is enough to make John L. Sullivan and Bob Fitzsimmons squirm in their graves.

On the whole it seems to have reached a stage where there is need of regulations to regulate the regulators.
