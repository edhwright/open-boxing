# Eastern Boxing News and Comment

New York City.—Kid Kaplan, the latest sensation in the 130-pound ranks, was a winner over Kid Sullivan after twelve rounds of rough milling at the Garden, Nov. 24th. Sullivan was just a bit too slow for the Meriden, Conn., battler.

* * *

Andy Thomas, after suffering a knock down in the opening round, came fast and won the verdict over Joe Welling in the second half of the double windup. The boys put up a wonderful battle for twelve rounds and will probably be rematched.

* * *

Pete Zivic defeated Kid Lewis in the opening four-rounder and Joe Frisco was a winner over Tibby Watson in six rounds. The show only drew $21,000, which is a small gate for the Garden.

* * *

Floyd Johnson wants it known that he is ready for Farmer Lodge and all that is necessary is for some club to come to the front with a fair offer. Floyd may box Bill Brennan at the Garden in December or early January.

* * *

Harvey Bright made a big hit at the Olympia, Philadelphia, November 20th, when he defeated Earl Hartman, one of the best of the Quaker City feathers. The boys went the scheduled eight rounds with the Fighting Doctor always in front. Harvey scored the only knockdown of the battle sending his man down for the count of nine in the seventh round.

* * *

Harry Greb and Gene Tunney are signed to box at the Garden, December 29th. The Pittsburger has announced that he will manage his own affairs after his contract expires with George Engel. Bad judgment on Harry's part, as there have been few fighters that could successfully manage their ring affairs. It is to be regretted that the two boys could not hit it up together. Engel is a straightforward chap and knows the game thoroughly. He points to the fact that Greb has made over $100,000 under his management. Probably between now and the time the contract expires the boys will patch things up and go on as usual.

* * *

Charley Pitts and George Chaney put up a sensational scrap at the Olympia, Philadelphia, November 20th. Knockout George was expected to make short work of Pitts but the latter was right in there at the end of the eighth round battling the Baltimore boy and giving as good as he received.

* * *

Midget Smith and Pancho Villa at 118 pounds is a match that is in the making and will in all probability take place at the Garden during January. The Midget is in good form again and that means that all the bantams will have their work cut out for them.

* * *

Manager Joe Wagner has had Jack Sharkey re-instated. This great little battler was set down for one year but Joe had it lifted in less than three months and expects to keep his charge busy from now on and as Jack has promised to keep in good shape the boys should get plenty of profitable engagements.

* * *

Frank Churchill still demands $25,000 for Pancho Villa to box Frankie Genaro. This seems exorbitant in view of the fact that Genaro has twice defeated the little Filipino.

* * *

Jack Delaney, the Bridgeport, Conn., battler is back in shape and will tackle Knockout Johnny King at Waterbury, Conn., the first week in December.

* * *

Sonny Smith is still waiting for Midget Smith to accept his challenge and several clubs want the match. Wake up, Harry Neary.

* * *

Mickey Walker, welterweight champion, has been doing a little stage work but will get busy after January 1st. Jimmy Kelly, the New York boy who battled Jack Britton at Havana, Cuba, will be his first opponent.

* * *

Wallie Jackson, fighting his first battle under the management of Sammy Goldman, made a great hit at the Olympia, Philadelphia, Nov. 20th, when he fought Johnny Mealy eight fast rounds.

* * *

The Boxing Commission refused to allow a Pancho Villa-Terry Martin match at 116 pounds, and ordered Villa to confine his efforts to the flyweight class.

* * *

Johnny Dundee, junior lightweight champion easily earned the judges' decision over Phil Delmont in 12 rounds at the Broadway Exhibition association club, New York City, Nov. 27th. Delmont was willing, but was outclassed all the way. They weighed in at 135.

* * *

Pancho Villa, American flyweight champion went out of his class when he defeated Young Montreal, bantamweight in ten rounds at Boston, Nov. 27th. Villa won eight rounds, Montreal one and one was even.

* * *

In Philadelphia they are singing the praises of Eddie (Kid) Wagner, who seeks a match with Johnny Dundee for the 130-pounds championship. Wagner started as a newsboy, as did Lew Tendler when he was a youngster. He hustled papers around the Broad Street station in Philly and knocked out a few of the kiddies who tried to get his stand. Maxie Hoff saw he had some fistic prospects and signed him to a contract. Wagner has had over 75 fights in his pro career, defeating some of the best boys at his weight. He can make the featherweight limit of 126 pounds but his manager claims he can't get him any work in that division.

* * *

Eddie and Jess McMahon are turning them away at their Commonwealth Club, since the cool weather started.
