# Boxing News of the World

## If You Don't Find It in the Blade It's Because It Isn't Much

Dan Lackey, Oklahoma City promoter, will stage his Jim Delaney-Harry Greb light heavyweight title bout during a gala celebration at the Oklahoma capital early in January. The festivities will mark the inauguration of Governor-elect J. O. Walton and the Oklahoma folks say it will be one of the biggest gatherings in history.

The inauguration will be held at the state fair grounds in view of everyone who wishes to see it and reports from Oklahoma City say 300,000 persons are expected to assemble for the occasion. A big barbecue will be a feature of the celebration.

Lackey has proven himself a capable promoter in the boxing field and the Oklahoma Cityans evidently think very highly of his ability to promote other events for they have placed him in charge of the inaugural arrangements.

The Delaney-Greb bout will add much color to the occasion and Lackey will undoubtedly be forced to "turn 'em away." Even as an attraction of its own, Dan expected, judging from advance sale and the enthusiasm, that it would draw a record attendance for bouts in the Southwest. After the injury of Delaney's hand forced the postponement from Nov. 17, the original date, to Dec. 15, Lackey decided to hold it over until the celebration.

### Jess Willard Would Fight Floyd Johnson

Jess Willard, former heavyweight champion of the world, evidently despairing of getting a promised return match with Jack Dempsey which has failed to materialize, is reported to have expressed his willingness to take on Floyd Johnson, the Iowa heavyweight. Johnson got his ring start in California and is now something of a sensation in New York where he has scored several wins the most notable of which was stopping Bob Martin. Willard also says he is in fine condition and "will surprise a lot of wise guys." If he gets another fight with Dempsey it will surprise more than that.

### Cliff Kramer Wants Revenge on Lodge

Word comes from California that Cliff Kramer, former St. Paul heavyweight, has fought himself out of opponents on the Coast and yearns to return to Minnesota seeking new fighters to conquer. He specifically avers that he would like a chance to avenge his defeat by Farmer Lodge, who knocked him out in seven rounds at St. Paul. Kramer is reported to have added a lot of weight during his stay on the Coast and now fights around 220. This makes him almost as big as Lodge who states that he will be glad to accommodate Kramer, any time, any place.

### Rickard Can't Get Yankees' New Stadium

Indications are that Tex Rickard will not be able to realize his plan to stage a fight between Jack Dempsey and Harry Wills at the new stadium of the New York American League ball club. Rickard is said to have obtained the consent of the owners of the Yankees for the use of the stadium which seats 100,000. Later, however, it was reported that they had been "scared off" the proposition through fear that if they permitted the park to be used for boxing the tax on baseball games would be increased to equal that on boxing contests which is five per cent. If Rickard wishes to stage the Dempsey-Wills fight on June 30th as planned, he will have to take it to his own stadium at Boyles acres, Jersey City.

### Villa Wants $25,000 to Fight Genaro

The proposed fight between Pancho Villa, American flyweight champion, and Frankie Genaro at Jersey City has been called off. Genaro's manager said that Villa had demanded a guarantee of $25,000 for a decision bout with Genaro in New York.

### Billy Murray Near Death in Arizona

"Fighting Billy" Murray, who a few years ago was one of the leading middleweights, is reported dying of tuberculosis in Arizona. Murray was brought out by Jack Kearns before Kearns "found" Jack Dempsey, and he fought all of the first class middles and light heavies including Jack Dillon, Eddie McGoorty, Tom Gibbons and others. He gave Gibbons one of the hardest battles of Tom's career at Hudson, Wis., where Mike Collins promoted boxing. Murray's eyes failed him in 1918 and he retired from the ring.

### Clabby Wins Fight But Loses Decision

Jimmy Clabby, former great middleweight, ran afoul of one of those queer decision that are handed out to visiting boxers in Denver, Colo. Clabby boxed Denver Tommy Commiskey there recently and, according to the report of the Denver Express, won nine of the twelve rounds. The account goes on to say that Clabby easily outpointed Commiskey in all but three rounds, was vastly superior at infighting and mussed up Tommy continually with uppercuts and a fast left. Commiskey floored Clabby for the seven count when he landed a wild haymaker in the eleventh round. He was presented with the decision for that one lucky punch.

### Princeton Football Coach on Boxing Commission

William Roper, head football coach at Princeton and a member of the Philadelphia city council, has been appointed a member of the new city boxing commission. William Hollenback, former Penn football coach, was also named on the commission.

### One-Eyed Connelly Seeks New Gates to Crash

With his golf sticks, eight trunks and a secretary, One-Eyed Connelly, the famous touring gentleman, has checked into New Orleans for the winter. No turnstile at fight club or race track is safe.

Scandal attended One-Eye's departure from New York on the eve of the world series, the supreme crash of the regular sporting season for crashers of high and low degree. Tammany Young, recognized champion crasher at all rates of admission, bribed One-Eye to take it on the leg from here, thereby removing his principal rival for the championship.

When he should have been tricking the gate-men the Polo grounds, One-Eye was taking easy workouts busting the contemptible little entrance to the race track in Cleveland. About six weeks ago he checked into the Indianapolis Times office and raised a riot at Eddie Ashe's deck. Jeff Davis, whilom king of the hoboes, was doing his monologue in Eddie's ear and One-Eye talked them both unconscious.

Connelly was next heard from at Lade park track, near Henderson, Ky., taking the gate every day in easy fashion. He seemed to be in great form to tackle one of Tex Rickard's big shows.

"Well, here I am," he writes now from New Orleans. "I got a secretary named Cinders and he looks like he might be a comer. We get into town on the Southern's best train from Birmingham. I'm going to learn him all about crashing. They use rotten coal down South. We aren't cleaned up yet but I aim to promote us a bath next week."

### Everything Goes in Argentina Boxing

Buenos Aires, Argentina.—Prize fights are barred by law in the Argentine, but nevertheless prize fights are held and a fighter isn't disqualified because he bites, scratches or pulls hair, if the affair is before a local referee.

A Buenos Aires boxer appeared recently before his friends with his face disfigured, the wounds indicating that a set of good strong teeth had found lodgment there. He said he had given an exhibition match and that his opponent had proceeded to bite him in the face.

"And what did you do?" he was asked.

"I bit him on the shoulder."

There has been only one big fight in the Argentine—that between the heavyweights Luis Firpo and Jim Tracey—but the local fights are frequent and howlingly patronized.

The Argentine takes his prize fight with a great deal of earnestness and the noise attending a contest reaches the proportion of a full-grown riot.

An unpopular fight or the opponent of a favorite needs all the police protection he can get, for the crowds will come storming against the ropes on very little provocation.

### Jim Tracey Back From South America

Jim Tracey, Australian heavyweight, who went to South America to fight Luis Angel Firpo, Argentine champion, and was knocked out in their bout at Buenos Aires, has arrived back in the states. Tracey's next start in this country will be against Homer Smith at Kalamazoo, Mich., on Dec. 15th.

### Miami Has Boxing Despite Anti-Fight Law

Although prize fighting is illegal in Florida under an old state law, boxing shows are permitted in Miami, where all city officials occupy ringside seats at the bouts. Ed Douglass is the promoter, and he stages two shows weekly throughout the winter season from Dec. 1st to April 15th. He holds his shows in the Armory which seats 1,200 and he draws from $1,000 up on each card. Promoter Douglass has shown some of the best boxers of the East and the South. He writes the Boxing Blade that the anti-prize fight law was an aftermath of the Jim Corbett-Charlie Mitchell world's championship battle at Jacksonville many years ago and that nobody believes in it. Miami has a boxing commission appointed by the city manager, who takes the place of a mayor in that city. Douglass has been promoting boxing and wrestling there for four years.

### This Bantam Issues Defi to All in Class

Byron Foutts of Canton, Ohio, manager of Frankie Portogallo, bantamweight, wants the boxing world to know that his boy challenges any bantam in the business, and so he sent his defi to the Boxing Blade for publication. Portogallo, who meets George Parr, featherweight, at Canton, Dec. 8th, takes them all on from 118 to 124 pounds.

### Boxing Shows Draw Good in Indianapolis

Nate Forb, matchmaker for the American Athletic club at Indianapolis, has signed Louie Lavelle, Anderson, Ind., lightweight, with Jimmy Dalton of Indianapolis for ten rounds on Dec. 12th. He writes that his shows are drawing good houses.

### Jimmie Potts in Charge of Amateur Show

Jimmie Potts, veteran Minneapolis boxer and referee, has been placed in charge of the amateur boxing show which the Giantvalley post of the American Legion will stage at the citizens club, Dec. 6th. Potts plans to card twelve bouts. A. R. Morshare of the Union State bank will handle the business.

### Bud Taylor Wins Over Billy O'Brien

Bud Taylor, Terre Haute, Ind., bantamweight, defeated Billy O'Brien of Duluth, Minn., in an eight-round bout at the Sportsmen's club at Peoria, Ill., Nov. 27th. Taylor took every round by a wide margin.
