# European Boxing News and Gossip

*Special Cable to Boxing Blade.*

London, England. — Bartley Madden, American heavyweight, won the decision on points over Guardsman Penwill, former British army heavyweight, in a fifteen-round bout here Nov. 27th. Madden proved too tough and fast for the English fighter.

* * *

London, Eng.—Dick Smith's win over George Cook by a knockout in the 15th round had the swell members of the N. S. C. behaving like school children. Smith has stepped into the place left vacant by Bombadier Wells. Of course, Wells will always be a certain attraction but his recent wretched performances have lost him many, many admirers. Cook fought well, in fact, quite up to the best he has shown over here. Yet he could do nothing with the 37-year-old ex-amateur. Smith boxed coolly and in the typical old English style, as pictured by Charlie Mitchell, Tom Sayers, and others of a by-gone era. The Australian's lack of punching power is still evident, Smith even beating him in this. I doubt if Smith will beat Beckett, who with all his faults, has a wallop and can box more than a little. Smith will naturally gain confidence by continued success and I see no reason why he should not win a few more fights before he packs his trunks away for good.

* * *

The Australians lost a bunch of money in bets. They were banking on the belief that their luck had turned. Lloyd put them in this train by a couple of wins, one down in Wales over Llewellyn, Jim Driscoll's "find" and another against Phil Suffling at the Ring Arena. Further support of their "luck" theory came from some good wins at the races.

* * *

Phil Suffling at one time was touted as a "white hope" and was a discovery of Horatio Bottomley, the member of Parliament who was sentenced to seven years imprisonment for fraud recently. Bottomley is one of the cleverest men in Great Britain and had he chosen to run straight could have reached a very high position in this country. He had a wonderful knowledge of the law which kept him out of its clutches for years. When he was running his paper "John Bull," he started a big training camp for boxers. The published idea was to turn out real champions at all weights, actually, it was nothing more than an advertising stunt. No one of any account came out from the camp.

Suffling, a huge muscle-bound fellow, won a competition at the ring Arena, but failed to do anything afterwards.

* * *

The lightweight division promises to promote some good battles. Seaman Hall, the title holder, has done little to enthuse over since he wrested the title from Ernie Rice. He lost to Alex Ireland a couple of weeks back and at Premierland recently could only share the honors with Harry Mason. The latter match was made at 2 lbs. over the title limit so that the Seaman crown was not involved, but whilst Mason scaled only 131½ lbs., all declined to weigh admitting overweight and paid forfeit. It was a poor bout. Hall appeared fully 14 lbs. heavier than the Provincial who had the best of the earlier rounds. He made the champion miss repeatedly and boxing much the faster scored quickly with lefts to the face. At the half way stage the Seaman's extra poundage began to tell and though not punching with any great power he did just enough to earn a draw at the finish of the 20 rounds. On this showing Hall is likely to lose his title when he fixes up with Johnny Brown, the Scotch champion. There are others in the running for the 135 lbs. title, notably Fred Archer and Alf Simmons. It should be noted that Hall did not train for his engagement with Mason, a fact that may have had a lot to do with his mediocre display.

* * *

As most people expected Walter Rossi failed to stop Eugene Criqui's winning streak over in Paris. Two and a half minutes sufficed to dispose of the Welshman's claims to title honors. Criqui flooring Rossi soon after the start for a count of 9. On rising Rossi stood up for a toe-to-toe slam and caught a left hook on the point for the full count.

* * *

It is stated here that Jack Dempsey is coming across again. What for? The only hope of a Siki bout lies in Siki beating Beckett. An unlikely happening in my opinion. He might get a few vaudeville engagements that would not pay his expenses out and home. Carpentier is out of the question and I don't think the writers here are enthusiastic over a Beckett-Dempsey match.

* * *

Mick McTigue opened his European campaign with a success at the expense of Johnny Basham. Johnny has passed out of the title class and ought to retire from the game. His three beatings by Ted Lewis convinced most that, though still retaining his great boxing skill his stamina has gone. Against McTigue he exhibited glimpses of his cleverness, but failed to stand up to the hard punching of the Irish-American, who appears to be a good class middleweight and one who might extend the all-conquering Ted Lewis.

* * *

Jim Driscoll's heavyweight discovery is another "frost." He failed to beat Albert Lloyd who has been defeated by nearly all the second raters here. Driscoll threw in the towel in the 14th round to save his boy further punishment.

* * *

Tommy O'Brien of Los Angeles has sent a challenge to the sporting papers here to meet Seaman Hall. Nothing is likely to come of it unless O'Brien actually comes here. He could get several bouts with Seaman Hall, Johnny Brown, Fred Archer, Mike Honeyman and others, but the financial end would probably not be so good as he gets in America, after taking out transportation, training, etc.
