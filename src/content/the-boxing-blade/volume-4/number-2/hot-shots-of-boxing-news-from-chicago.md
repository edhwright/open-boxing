# Hot Shots of Boxing News from Chicago

Sensations! Well, Jack Eile, "The Fighting Fool," created one of these the other night when he substituted on a one day's notice and put up the greatest battle witnessed around these parts in years and he gave Eddie Anderson the toughest ten rounds that Anderson has encountered since his invasion of Chicago, and Eddie has boxed Mike Dundee and Joe Burman in this vicinity. Eile gave him plenty that he will remember for quite a while and only Anderson's better condition told in the closing rounds. Manager Larney Litchenstien immediately signed Jack to box Joe Burman here on Dec. 15.

* * *

Charley White! I'll take $25,000," says Richie Mitchell in N. Y. headline. This was the Milwaukee battler's demand when an offer was made by Tex Rickard for the Garden. Richie claims that White is as good as Leonard and is more dangerous and that is what he received when he boxed the Champ in that famous contest.

* * *

Weekly visitors were Tommy O'Brien, who defeated K. O. Jaekle and the latter's manager, Sam Jordon. Matchmaker John Wagner of Kenosha, Johnny Tannor of Minneapolis on his way to Detroit, Chet Koppel and Otto Borchert, scribe and promoter, who were down from the Cream City to see O'Brien box.

* * *

Jock Malone, boxing one of the preliminaries,—sounds strange, but it is true. Malone was asked by Promoter Jim Mullen to referee the Eile and Anderson contest and when the St. Paul battler heard that his many friends were going to present him with a diamond-studded belt, Jock insisted on boxing for his numerous friends in that vicinity. He scored a four-round knockout over Jack Duffy and refused to accept any pay for his services.

* * *

Vincent Porkoni, the Cleveland welter, who has boxed Lew Tendler, has arrived in town and was matched by his manager, Maxey Lando, to trade punches with the promising Mickey Forkins at a private club. Porkoni in his workouts has shown good form and, if he gets over the Forkin's hurdle he will seek matches with Sailor Friedman and Morrie Schlaiffer.

* * *

Harold Smith, can be called a junior bantam, because he is too heavy for Pancho Villa and too light for Joe Lynch, according to the N. Y. "Comish" new ruling. They say no one can have any weight on Pancho, but Manager Buck Montgomery will send him East just the same after his bout with Pal Moore. Smith only weighs 114 pounds but takes them all on.

* * *

Ever Hammer, after a hard season with Lew Tendler and Benny Leonard, will again don the mitts when Manager George Ferguson will take the blonde battler to Cincinnati to box K. O. Mars. Ferguson has mapped out a campaign that will put Ever back in the limelight he was in when in his last come-back stunt he defeated Charley White and Richie Mitchell.

* * *

Tommy O'Brien, who showed here the other night with K. O. Jaekle, has been offered a date with Johnny Dundee in Milwaukee for Dec. 29, when his suspension ends in Wisconsin. Tommy has an offer from a Boston promoter for three battles at $3,000 a fight, which the former coast boxer is considering. O'Brien speaks well of a youngster from the coast, Ernie Gooseman, whom he will bring out this way and wants to match him with the best featherweights.

* * *

Sammy Mandell took another step forward when he outpointed the clever Joey Sanger in Milwaukee last week. Promoter Otto Borchert is trying to arrange a Lynch match for Mandell. His style is a repetition of Benny Leonard and all he needs is the experience.

* * *

Sailor Friedman signed a contract with John Wagner, who runs his shows in Kenosha and depends on the Chicago crowd. Wagner was in town last week and is after the best looking match at the present time. Wagner has wired to Leo P. Flynn for Dave Shade to be Friedman's opponent but up to the present writing there isn't any answer as yet. Sailor is working daily for his bout with Pete Latzo in Philadelphia on Dec. 11 and he will appear in a bout with Special Delivery Hirsh for a local church, gratis.

* * *

Davey Miller, the Windy City referee, has offers from all parts of the country to officiate which he is taking to the best of his advantage. At present, Miller is official referee at Omaha, Davenport, two Chicago clubs. He will handle the Greb and Delaney battle in Oklahoma City on Dec. 17.
