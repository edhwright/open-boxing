# Editorial

The past few weeks there have been press reports sent out of New York and circulated throughout the country to the effect that European boxers were side stepping the boxing game in New York by demanding unreasonable terms, particularly quoting the terms demanded by Beckett, Siki, Wilde and Carpentier—as all out of order compared with what same boxers receive in Europe. As an explanation of these demands of foreign boxers, we refer to an editorial written by Billy Rocap, Sporting Editor of the Philadelphia Ledger, same we think is a just explanation as follows:

> There is wailing and gnashing of teeth among the New York promoters of boxing. Cablegrams to Carpentier, Beckett and Wilde offering them luring purses to compete in the United States have been ignored. It is a puzzle to the promoters why Carpentier should refuse $150,000 to box in this country and meet Siki in a Paris ring for a sum less than $25,000. It should be no puzzle to those who have studied the art of the money separators. By the time Carpentier would meet the tax in this country, then the tribute demanded by France and pay financial homage to the hangers on his $150,000 would soon dwindle. The big purses held out by American promoters do not look so large when viewed from the other end of the binoculars.
>
> Let us just take Jimmy Wilde as an example. When he was in this country three years ago he cleaned up $75,000. He had eleven contests. In comparison a lean reward as world's champions go—for Wilde was a real champion. Although he is thrifty, bordering on the penurious, when he sailed away he scarcely took half that amount back with him to Wales. He was separated from some of his ring earnings in the most despicable way.
>
> **Let us just cite one. A dinner was given in New York in his honor. It was a great handshaking occasion. Everybody was eager to greet the little Welshman. Several days later he received a bill for the dinner. He was told to pay it or he would be panned. It being his first time in America, he thought it the custom and paid it.**
>
> Now, you do not wonder why Wilde, Carpentier or Beckett do not rush to the steamship whenever they receive a cable to box in America. The offered purse looks big, but when the different tolls are exacted it dwindles in size and the gate at home does not suffer in comparison. We do not read about the exodus of any great American boxers to Europe. Kilbane made a short visit there, but did not compete. Dempsey has had several offers to compete in London and Paris from responsible promoters, but in each instance side-stepped the proposition. International ring competition is at ebb.
