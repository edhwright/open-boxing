# Great Falls Boxing Commission Sample of What All Official Bodies Should Be For Good of Game

## Members Are Big Men Who Know All Angles of the Sport and Who Treat All Connected With Game On the Square—Chairman Dan Tracy Is Old Time Boxer Who Gives Fighters a Fair Deal—All Members Competent Officials and Real Fellows.

The Boxing Commission at Great Falls, Mont., is a credit to its members and to the Boxing Game.

After some more or less funny experiences with boxing commissioners in some parts of the country, it is a relief and a pleasure to be able to pay a deserving tribute to such a body of boxing officials.

The Montana commission plan is different from that in most states. Each county in which there are cities where boxing shows are staged has its own commission.

The Cascade county commission supervises shows in Great Falls. This commission is composed of men who know the boxing game, inside and outside the ropes. Moreover, they are men who treat fairly all connected with the game. Fans, promoters, managers and boxers are treated on the square. All are treated alike, there is no favoritism shown anybody. In particular, they regard the managers and boxers as important parts of boxing and show them the consideration they have coming. They harbor no prejudices against boxers and managers.

This is more than can be said for some boxing commissions, the members of which seem to look on the boxers and managers as something to be merely tolerated as necessary evils and who jump at any chance to persecute them on the slightest pretext, often without just grounds.

### A Real Commission

As manager of Jim Delaney, whom I had at Great Falls for a bout recently, I came in contact with the Cascade County Commission in line with my professional duties. I want to record here that never during my long connection with boxing have I ever done business with a more efficient group of officials or a finer set of men.

As I said before, the commission is composed of men who are interested in boxing as a sport, not as a political plaything to be manipulated for their own private interests. Their official acts are for the good of boxing and they do what they can to give the sport a boost.

The members of the commissions are Dan Tracy and Loy J. Molumby of Great Falls and Jay Leland of Belt, Montana.

Tracy is president of the Great Falls Smelting and Mining Co. He is a big man in every way, as a business man, boxing official and real fellow. Furthermore, Tracy was once a boxer himself. He fought many of the leading heavyweights in his time and was good enough in the art to be matched with Bob Fitzsimmons, former champion of the world. Obviously, he knows boxing from the inside, and can properly appreciate the boxer's position in his official rulings. It is too bad there are not more men like him on boxing commissions.

Molumby is a Great Falls attorney and state commander of the American Legion. He is a graduate of the University of Minnesota, where he was a noted athlete and a fine amateur boxer. He is a great admirer of boxing and has been a boxing fan all his life.

Leland is sheriff of his home county and a retired stockman.

All in all the members of the Cascade County Commission are a fine bunch of clean cut, real men, and the boxing game would be better off if there were more like them in official capacities.

### Promoter is Regular Fellow

Frank E. McDonnell, promoter and matchmaker at Great Falls is a business man, who has been a follower of boxing all his life, and who knows the game from A to Z. He also is a regular fellow and square shooter.

Pete Foley, one of the judges, is a fine sportsman and a highly competent official. There would be fewer funny decisions on boxing bouts if there were more like him.

Bouts are decided in Great Falls by the referee and two judges. This system has not always been satisfactory in some states where it is in vogue owing to incompetent officials. However, if men who know the game were appointed, as in Great Falls, it would work out all right.

On the afternoon of the contest the commission holds a meeting at which the boxers, referee, seconds and everyone in any way connected with the show is present. Dan Tracy is the chairman of the commission at this meeting, and he minces no words in letting the boxers know what is expected of them. The boxer is given to understand that he must do his best, but he is also assured that he is of some account, and the boxer leaves the meeting with the understanding that although he can not get away with anything crooked the commissioners know enough about the game to judge his efforts honestly. They are also warned that no second of questionable character will be allowed in the corner. This does away with the possibility of anything crooked being pulled off on a strange fighter.

### Delaney Works Like Champ

In winding up this article I wish to say something about the way Jim Delaney performed at Great Falls. He never looked better than he did in his fight with Eddie Richards, a hard puncher and tough fighter from Oregon. Delaney had everything. He knocked Richards down in the fifth and when Eddie got up, Jim coolly measured him and knocked him out like a champion. If Delaney fights like he did that night when he meets Harry Greb, there will be a new American light heavyweight champion. He boxed cleverly and hit hard and accurately, making Richards look like a novice.

Delaney was unfortunate in hurting his right hand early in the bout, when he socked Richards on the elbow with an uppercut. But despite this handicap he won as he pleased and showed that he is a two-handed puncher by putting his man away with a left hook after cutting him down with snappy left jabs and jarring left hooks to the body. Delaney made a big hit and can go back to Great Falls any time he wants to.
