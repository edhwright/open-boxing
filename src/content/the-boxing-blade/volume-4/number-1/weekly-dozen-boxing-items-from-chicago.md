# Weekly Dozen Boxing Items From Chicago

1.—Sailor Friedman's sensational work of late indicates that the west side boxer has taken on a new lease of life. With knockouts over Eddie Fitzsimmons and K. O. Laughlin he wants a bout with Mickey Walker, the Welter King. Friedman is back home training for his battle with Bobby Barrett on Thanksgiving afternoon in Philadelphia.

2.—One of the Chicago newspapers is running a Christmas Fund for kiddies and a number of the local boxers and managers have donated. During a fanning bee at which the boxers tried to out bid each other, Pal Moore, Harold Smith, Bud Taylor, Sammy Mandell, and Jack Eile, agreed to give a percentage of their income from all bouts between now and Christmas. Another boost to the sport.

3.—Jock Malone battles Bryan Downey at Louisville next week.

4.—Eddie Anderson back from New York with a draw decision with Terry Martin has tied up for three bouts, Irish Johnny Curtin, New York; Young Montreal, Detroit; and Patsy Flanigan, Chicago. Anderson being in the same stable with Joe Burman, has caused the boys to try harder for the championship goal. Burman is working out daily at the Arcade Gym.

5.—Kid Howard's interest in amateur boxing is a great boost to the sport. His tournaments are held every week at his Arcade gym and a lot of promising boxers are developed. The best prospects will be sent to other cities to represent Chicago, in the A. A. U. contests. At present Joey Ray, star middle distance runner, has shown the best form and is devoting his time only to boxing.

6.—Mike Dundee will again journey to New York, only this time his manager, Dick Curley has a contract for three battles calling for regular money.

7.—Larney Lichtenstein has his two battlers signed up for a few bouts, Jimmy Clabby, his middleweight will box Tommy Commiskey at Denver, while Jack Eile, his junior featherweight, will face Eddie Anderson at Chicago and Joe Berger at Denver.

8.—Nate Lewis was back in town after a year's stay in New York. He closed some important matters with his partner, Tom Walsh, regarding matches for both Jock Malone and Joe Burman, then hiked back to Broadway.

9.—Pal Moore, Chicago bantam star, has offers from all parts of the country to box a lot of promising youngsters who think that Moore has gone back, but they will be fooled as Pal is boxing in old time form as he showed by his decisive victory over Frankie Jummatti. He now is matched to box the sensational Harold Smith here on Thanksgiving day.

10.—Weekly visitors were Tommy Murray and Pete Moran from Philadelphia, Joey Sanger and his brother who came down from Milwaukee to work out at the Arcade Gym, Babe Asher, K. O. Jeakle, and Tommy O'Brien, who will box on the U. S. S. Commodore, Bryan Downey, Billy Wells, Frankie Mason, Frankie Schoell and Sid Bernard, the Brooklyn schoolboy on his way to the coast.

11.—Bud Taylor's manager, Eddie Long, has an offer to box Frankie Genaro, who has two decisions over Pancho Villa. Bud is a real classy boxer, only weighing around the flyweight limit but has been boxing bantams.

12.—Audre Anderson, Chicago heavyweight, has been working out every day for a month. Audre would like a tangle with Farmer Lodge any distance, any place, any time.
