# Boxing News of the World

## If You Don't Find It in the Blade It's Because It Isn't Much

Pancho Villa, champion flyweight of America and the Orient, has proved himself one of the greatest little fighters we have had here in some time. He is a native Filipino and scales only 108 pounds, but he has demonstrated his mastery over the best at the weight here and even up to 116 pounds. When his manager, Frank Churchill of Manila, first brought him to the states last spring, there were few who would even give him a hearing for his protege. Now they are clamoring after his services. Mr. Churchill had faith in the lad and the latter has proved conclusively that it was not misplaced. His battle with Abe Goldstein in New York, giving away over six pounds in weight, again proved his worth. Villa won all the way and made Mr. Abe stick to the rear end of the procession. Before the contest Goldstein was a favorite in the betting, owing to the advantage in weight and the fact that he had shaded the Filipino when they met some months ago. Villa, however, made up for his former showing by leading in every round and winning the verdict without a murmur from any source. Tex Rickard has tried to get Jimmy Wilde, world's flyweight champion, over from England for a battle with Pancho, but to date has not been successful. Wilde has stated that he will be pleased to meet Villa in Europe, but will not journey at present to the states for a title match.

### Boxing Shows Draw Poorly in Twin Cities

The Minneapolis boxing show that had been scheduled for Nov. 17th between Mike Dundee and Eddy Anderson was cancelled. Dundee first said he cut his lip and later claimed that he hurt his hand—anyhow no one was substituted. The promoters likely figured that they would be farther ahead to cancel the show entirely, which they did.

It is right now questionable whether or not there will be another boxing show staged in Minneapolis this year. The profits to date in Minneapolis have been nothing and according to the small crowd that attended the last St. Paul show, St. Paul is running along in about the same class.

Amateur shows are thriving. Also wrestling is about to be started in the Twin Cities.

### Bobby Harper Beats Davis for Coast Title

Bobby Harper of Portland, Oregon, won the decision over Travie Davis of Seattle, Washington, on Nov. 10th. The bout was for the welterweight championship of the Pacific Coast. What won for Harper was his savage in-fighting, as Davis had the better of the going at long range boxing. Davis' worst round was the 10th in which Harper had him in a bad way during the entire round. Harper weighed 146, Davis 146½.

* * *

### Sailor Tom King Shades Bat Ortega

Sailor Tom King won a shade decision over Battling Ortega at Portland, Oregon, in 10 rounds on Nov. 14th. King waded in from the first bell and for the first four rounds had the edge. In the fifth Ortega landed a rain of rights and lefts on King's face and jaw. For a minute it looked like King would take the count but he pulled through and finished the balance of the fight with margin enough to make Referee Louttit hold up his hand. It was a good fight.

### Panama Joe Gans Beats Marty Cross

Panama Joe Gans, who holds the Tex Rickard welter and middleweight championship belt, shaded Martin Cross, hard hitting New York welterweight at New York, Nov. 18th. It was a slam bang battle from start to finish.

* * *

### Jeff Smith Has Had 623 Bouts in Ring

According to Al Lippe, Jeff Smith has had 623 bouts in the ring. In checking up to this total Smith has included all bouts and exhibitions for charity. Jeff says that he feels no bad effects of his many bouts. He is 29 years old and has faced opponents in most countries of the globe outside of South America. Smith has taken on all of the best and has never been stopped.

* * *

### Sammy Mandell Gets Shade Over Joey Sangor

Sammy Mandell won shade decision over Joey Sangor in ten rounds at Milwaukee, Wis., Nov. 20th. There were no knock downs. Sammy Craden, junior lightweight of Milwaukee defeated Charley Glaser in the eight round semi-windup.

### Billy Fox Runs Omaha Sportsman's Hangout

Billy Fox, prominent Nebraska sportsman, has bought out the Sportsman's Cigar Store at Omaha. Fox is one of the real dyed-in-the-wool boxing fans. His place will be a headquarters for boxing and boxers. All bouts of prominence are re-fought at the Sportsman's Cigar store daily. Billy is a regular fellow.

* * *

### Benny Valger vs. Willie Ames on Newsboys' Benefit Show

Bernie Strapp, acting as matchmaker for local sportsmen who are staging a benefit for the newsboys, at Akron, Ohio, Dec. 7th, has matched up Benny Valger, the French flash, now working out of New York with Willie Ames of Akron, to feature the show in a ten-round bout. There will be a star set of preliminaries put on the same bill that will be announced later.

* * *

### Billy Wells Beats Morrie Schlaifer

Billy Wells proved too much for Morrie Schlaifer in their ten-round argument at Davenport, Iowa, Nov. 17. Wells popped Schlaifer with straight lefts and rights all through the fight. Morrie's right eye was nearly closed and his mouth cut up. Wells beat Schlaifer in the clinches and at long range boxing, all of Morrie's swings landing on Wells' arms and shoulders. Wells proved hard to hit. His boring in tactics puzzled Schlaifer while his superior speed and ring generalship was in evidence throughout the fight.

### Logan and Josephs Campaign on Coast

Bud Logan and Jack Josephs, star junior welterweights of the Gibbons-Collins stable, are in California where they will campaign in the four-round game at Oakland, Los Angeles, San Francisco and other spots in the Golden Gate state. Logan and Josephs will tackle the best boys in their class during their stay on the coast. Logan's first bout will be on Nov. 28th at Oakland and Josephs will get in action on Dec. 5th.

### Leo Flynn Moves New York Office

Leo P. Flynn has moved his office from the Gaiety Theatre building to the Putman building, New York City. Leo wanted to get close to Dan Morgan, so they say.

### Heavyweight Hope Tourney at Garden

James Joy Johnson will show an army of new heavyweight prospects at Madison Square Garden next Monday night. They will compete against each other to see who is who or if there is a who among them. They weigh from 180 to 240. Jimmy hopes to get an opponent for Dempsey out of the bunch.

### Amateur Show Billed in Minneapolis

The Grant Valley Post of the American Legion at Minneapolis will stage an amateur show at the Citizens' Club, December 6th. Billy Hoke is making the matches and A. R. Morshare will handle the business.

### Jim Delaney Ready Again for All Comers

James J. Delaney, St. Paul contender for the light heavyweight crown, is doing light training to keep in shape while his injured right hand is healing. Jim put that fist out of use in socking Eddie Richards at Great Falls, Mont., a few weeks ago. After disabling his right in an early round in that fight Delaney showed that he is a two-handed puncher by knocking out his tough opponent in the fifth with a left hook to the chin. The injury forced Delaney to postpone his try for the light heavyweight title on Nov. 17th when he was scheduled to box Harry Greb before promoter Dan Lackey's club at Oklahoma City, Okla. However, Delaney will likely get his big chance sometime the latter part of December. Meantime his right hand is healing nicely and he will again be ready for all comers in a week or so.

### All-Star Show Carded for Philadelphia

Jack Hanlon, matchmaker for the Arena (formerly the Ice Parlor), at Philadelphia, has lined up a snappy looking card for Thanksgiving afternoon. He will put on five eight-round bouts.

Bobby Barrett and Pal Moran will top the show. Other bouts will be Johnny Dundee with Aleck Hart, Joe Tiplitz vs. Shamus O'Brien, Babe Herman and Johnny Brown, Benny Bass and young Leonard.

### Billy Sterns Wins Over Perry Blivens

Billy Sterns, Minneapolis welterweight, gave Perry Blivens of Grand Forks, N. D. a good licking at Valley City, N. D., Nov. 7th. Sterns kept his left mitt in Blivens' face throughout the bout and the Grand Forks boy was badly punished.

### Blink McCloskey Trains Egyptian Prince

Blink McCloskey, former Philadelphia welterweight, who was in the states recently as secretary to the nephew of an Egyptian Prince, is back in Paris for a rest. Blink says it is harder acting as secretary to the Prince than fighting middleweights and heavies in the roped arena. It has been Blink's duty to train the Prince in athletics, etc., and act as interpreter. "I may not be a champion," said Blink, "but who is there among all the champions of the world, Jack Dempsey included, who could take the Prince in hand, teach him physical culture and act as interpreter for him, speaking English, French, Russian, Jewish and Egyptian?" We agree with Blink—there is no boxer outside of himself who could hold down such a job.

### Farmer Lodge Kayos Two in Three Days

Farmer Lodge has at last got going in big league style. The Farmer with his 230 pounds knocked out two men in three days last week.

Lodge's first victim was Jim Pappas, Montana heavyweight hope whom the Farmer knocked into a cocked hat in three rounds on Nov. 14th at Butte, Montana. The next on the list was Bud Sampson, whom the Farmer knocked completely out of business in three rounds at Valley City, North Dakota, on Nov. 17th.

Mike Gibbons took care of Lodge in both bouts and reports that the improvement in the Farmer is beyond imagination.

Says the Phantom, "He steps around like a lightweight, uses a lightning left and when his right goes over there is no question about anything, the bout is over."

Lodge has an offer from Otto Borchard at Milwaukee to box Floyd Johnson and another from Dayton, Ohio with Tut Jackson, and has accepted both bouts with the understanding that Floyd Johnson and Jackson go through.

### Holland Champion Challenges Siki

The Dutchmen of Holland think they have a heavyweight who can whip Battling Siki, the Senegalese who recently whipped Georges Carpentier. Before Siki became champion of Europe he was in Holland for a time and while there acted as sparring partner to the Dutch champion—Piet Van der Leer. The latter stands over six feet and weighs 199 pounds. He was beaten by Paul Journee, the French heavyweight, two years ago, but later came to the scratch and defeated Journee, also the Italian champion, Spalla, and Harry Reeves, the English light heavyweight. Siki, while in Holland, married a Dutch girl.
