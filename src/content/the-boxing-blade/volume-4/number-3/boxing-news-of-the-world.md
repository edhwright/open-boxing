# Boxing News of the World

## If You Don't Find It in the Blade It's Because It Isn't Much

Jimmy Dougherty, manager of Bobby Barrett, in Philadelphia writes that he is of the opinion that most of the lightweights are afraid to meet his boy.

"Bobby is the coming champion," said James, "and it will not be long before he is trailing Benny Leonard for a championship match. He had a few setbacks but that doesn't bother us any, as all fighters have had bad breaks at some time during their careers. Charley White scored a technical knockout over Bobby but he surely got a beating the next time they met. Bobby can whip White now any time they start and he will do the same to Lew Tendler before long. He is only a youngster yet, but developing fast and you can gamble he will trim the whole bunch before he gets through."

* * *

### Emke-Sheridan Bout Wanted at Sioux City

The Cudahy Athletic Club of Sioux City, Iowa, will stage a show December 16th. Promoter Costella is trying to sign up Billy Emke to box Homer Sheridan for the ten-round main event. Sioux City is a good boxing town.

* * *

### Lodge McLean on Next Duluth Card

Ray Carnes of Duluth will stage his next boxing show December 18. Carnes is angling for a good tough opponent for Farmer Lodge. Also he will likely use Stewart McLean in another 10-round bout. Both Lodge and McLean won their last starts in Duluth by knocking out their opponents.

* * *

### Boston Critic Likes Pancho Villa's Work

Pancho Villa, the flyweight champion from Filipino land, made a big hit in Boston when he whipped Young Montreal recently. Arthur Duffy writing in the Boston Post, says Pancho is the finest little piece of fighting machinery that he has ever seen rolled up in a 110-pound package and he raves about what a fight we would see with Pancho opposing Jimmy Wilde when the two little fellows were at their best.

* * *

### Siki Blow Was Legal, Films of Bout Show

Films of the Siki-Carpentier disaster were shown in Vancouver, B. C., recently and according to newspaper reports, they gave the fans plenty to talk about. The photography was excellent, the reports say, and every move of the two fighters was clearly shown.

The press also tells that the picture testifies against the contention of some critics that the Senegalese fouled Carpentier. The blow in question looks perfectly legal on the screen, they say.

* * *

### Tex Rickard Wants Firpo-Brennan Scrap

According to reports from New York, Tex Rickard would like to put on a bout between Luis Firpo and Bill Brennan and he is said to have cabled the South American asking him to return to Gotham. Rickard is quoted as saying that he figures Firpo has a good chance to beat Brennan and that the man who knocks Bill out will become the most logical Dempsey challenger. However, Tex adds that Luis may not care to clash with Brennan and he doubts that he will be able to close such a bout.

* * *

### Smith & Co. Expected to Abolish Judges

One of the many changes expected when the Smith administration revises the boxing rules in New York is the abolishing of the two judges, leaving only the referee to render the verdicts. The present system has been often criticized because of the apparent opportunity for collusion among the officials to give unfair decisions. Artie McGovern, one of the official referees of the state, resigned recently because of his belief that the present system is wrong.

* * *

### Bartfield Is Managing Billiard Hall, Gym

Soldier Bartfield, the veteran Brooklyn welterweight, has gone into the managing business but it is not as a manager of boxers. The Soldier has taken charge of a big billiard hall in Brooklyn and he has also opened up a gymnasium.

* * *

Dave Rosenberg drew "thirty days" for his fouling of Mike O'Dowd in their scrap at Brooklyn Thanksgiving day. Dave, according to the reports, was warned twice in the eighth round for hitting low and the disqualification followed. His end of the receipts were held up pending investigation.

* * *

### Fitzsimmons Arena Disposal Is Held Up

Government officials have decided to hold up the disposal of the $60,000 boxing arena erected by Floyd Fitzsimmons at Michigan City until the coming session of the Indiana legislature has passed. The decision followed a report that a boxing bill favored and supported by Governor McCray will be introduced during the session.

* * *

### Featherweight Sparkles on Card at Omaha

Erwin Bige, a featherweight, looked sweet to the Omaha scribes in the boxing show at the Cudahy Athletic club in South Omaha the other night. They say he pounded Harry Kinnear, a veteran Omahan, from corner to corner, and showed remarkable coolness. They predict a bright future for the boy. The main event of the night was a tame ten round bout between Harvey Thorpe and Johnny Nichols. It was called a draw.

* * *

### Greb Will Return to Mason, Report

Harry Greb will return to Reddy Mason following the termination of his contract with George Engel Dec. 30, they are saying in New York. Mason managed the light heavyweight champ for several years, before Greb won the American title.

* * *

### Genero-Villa Clash Off; Money Not Up

When Phil Bernstein called at a New York bank the other day and found that the promoter had not
posted the guarantees, he called off the twelve-round bout that would have brought his bantamweight, Frankie Genaro, together with Pancho Villa Dec. 13. According to the terms, the Villa guarantee was to be $4,500 with Genaro receiving $3,500.

* * *

### Rocky Kansas Makes a Brilliant Comeback

Rocky Kansas, the Buffalo lightweight, made a flashy comeback in his home city Thanksgiving day when he received the decision over Pete Hartley in ten rounds. The Italian, who was making his first appearance since his knockout defeat by Benny Leonard, displayed plenty of speed and his hitting power appeared to be as strong as ever.

* * *

### Gibbons and Miske to Box Again

Tommy Gibbons and Billy Miske will box again, for the fifth time, in St. Paul Dec. 15. It is expected that the bout will draw a good crowd. There seems no question but what Tommy will win, as usual. However, many Twin Cities fans will undoubtedly want to see Gibbons in action. The last bout between the two in the Twin Cities, held in Minneapolis, was nothing to go wild over, in fact it was very, very tame. But that was before Tommy was considered the goods.

* * *

### Brennan Expected to Refuse Go With Lodge

Farmer Lodge has signed to box Bill Brennan at Grand Rapids, Michigan for either the 15th or 18th of December. Mr. Morrissey, the Grand Rapids promoter, at this writing is still worried as to whether or not Brennan will accept Lodge, although Brennan said to get any one.

Three years ago when Lodge was only a preliminary boxer, and Brennan was at his best, Lodge took a bout with Brennan on a few days' notice and without training at Detroit, Michigan. In the first round, the Farmer hit Big Bill on the whiskers so hard that the punch almost knocked out Leo Flynn, who was in Brennan's corner. Brennan hit the floor and the referee counted over Bill up to nine, when the bell was the most welcome it ever had been at any time during Brennan's career, including his two bouts with Dempsey. At the end of three rounds the Farmer was all in for gas and was forced to accept the towel.

The Farmer now is a real fighter, a great boxer and the hardest hitter in the business. We will be surprised if Brennan accepts him any place.

* * *

### Dunn Has Promising Bantam; Goes East

Jimmie Dunn has a little bantam who is coming along in nice shape. He is Al Corbett, who has been acting as sparring partner for Carl Tremaine. According to Jimmy, Corbett is a good boxer and a pleasing fighter. He will make his first eastern start at Philadelphia next month before Jack Hanlon's Club. Great things are expected from little Corbett.

* * *

### Omaha to Have New Deputy Commissioner

There is to be a change in deputy boxing commissioners at Omaha. Not one has yet been appointed. There has been considerable friction between John Kilmartin, present inspector, and boxers, managers and some promoters. It is hoped that someone will be appointed who has a full understanding of the boxing business.

Either Gene Melady or Bernie Boyle could fill the position to the entire satisfaction of all who want a square deal. Both of the above mentioned men know the game thoroughly and would be a credit to the supervision of boxing in any state as well as Nebraska.

* * *

Olie Anderson, Minneapolis heavyweight, journeyed to Wichita, Kansas, to box Bob Roper, Nov. 28, but Bob did not show up and Olie took on Big Bill Steel, for 10 rounds. The first round, Steel gave Olie quite a fight. The second round, Steel kissed the canvas twice and the third he went down three times more, the bell saving him. The fourth session, Anderson landed a stiff right hook to Bill's jaw that put the Canadian down for keeps.

Anderson made such an impression with the fans that he was matched right back with Chief Metoqua of Hutchinson, Kansas, at the same club, Dec. 12th.

Mike Gibbons will accompany Anderson to Wichita. According to reports from Wichita the bout will draw one of the largest crowds ever attending a boxing show in Kansas.

* * *

### Logan Signs to Box Long or Joe Benjamin

Bud Logan, who is now in Los Angeles has accepted terms to box Ray Long or Joe Benjamin at Hollywood, Dec. 15th. Logan should give either Joe or Ray a merry run for their money. Bud is now at his best and knows the game backwards.

* * *

### Littleton and Downey Fight at New Orleans

"Happy" Littleton and Bryan Downey have been signed to meet in New Orleans on the night of Dec. 15th. It was Downey who knocked "Happy" out for the first and only time in the career of the southern middleweight. Many of Littleton's followers feel that he was overtrained for the bout and they are confident he will erase that black mark on his good looking record.

* * *

### Smith Does David Act; Whips Martin Burke

Jeff Smith, veteran middleweight, took Martin Burke into camp in a fifteen-round bout at New Orleans, Dec. 1st. Burke had ten pounds advantage in weight and about a foot in height but with all that Burke received a thorough licking. According to New Orleans newspaper reports, Jeff outfought, outboxed, outslugged and outsmarted Burke. Burke told his friends after the bout and he had no pep and could not "get going."
