# Editorial

George (Kid) Lavigne, one of the greatest lightweights America ever produced, and who was supreme about the year 1896, had a sad Thanksgiving day. On the day when most people were enjoying a turkey feast the famous "Saginaw Kid" was putting his wife to her last resting place at Saginaw, Mich., near where he was born 53 years ago. Mrs. Lavigne died two days before Thanksgiving day. It was the hardest punch the Kid ever received and one that will last.

There has been much discussion of late as to the relative merits of the stars of 30 years ago and those of today, especially between stars like the late Joe Gans, George Lavigne and Benny Leonard. Gans was a master at boxing and also carried a knockout in his right hand whenever he put it to the right spot. Lavigne was not the boxer that Gans was, but he was more hardy and could stand a terrific mauling without showing the least distress, but he could also hand it out, being a hard hitter with either hand. The Kid lost to Mysterious Smith in San Francisco in 1899 because he gave away too much weight and was not at his best then, having begun to go back. Benny Leonard, with his cleverness, and Lavigne, with his ruggedness, would have made a wonderful combination for a great fight and having seen both men in action it would be hard at this time to pick a winner. The Kid was very fast in his movements and that overcame greatly any lack of science, but his remarkable endurance and ability to take punishment was an off-set to any other advantage his opponents might have. In a long fight the Kid was always a pretty safe bet.
