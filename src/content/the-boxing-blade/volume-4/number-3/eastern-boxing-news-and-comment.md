# Eastern Boxing News and Comment

New York City—Gentlemen, meet Mike O'Dowd, middleweight champion again. Mike lambasted Dave Rosenberg all over the ring at the Rink Sporting Club, Brooklyn, Thanksgiving day. After winning easily for seven rounds, Mike was fouled in the eighth. The bout, scheduled to go fifteen rounds, ended just when it looked as if Michael was going to score a knockout. The N. Y. State Athletic Commission had designated Rosenberg as the title holder and winning over Rosey makes Mike the champion. He will at least meet all comers just as he did when he held the title before. He was and is a fighter that bars none and it is to be hoped that he holds the championship until a fighter like himself comes along, one that will not demand the earth to fight and will not try to pick set-ups. The house was about $7,000, all the arena could hold at the popular prices.

* * *

A new face has appeared on the fistic horizon in the person of Jack Bernstein of Yonkers, N. Y. This youngster is the best drawing card the New York clubs have had since the days of his namesake, Joe Bernstein. Every time he appears on a card the S. R. O. sign appears. Best of all, he wins and only recently won over Pal Moran of New Orleans. His most recent victory was over Babe Herman of California. The bout took place at the Pioneer Club, November 29 and it was an overflow. Bernstein took every one of the twelve rounds and the verdict. The gate was over $5,000, which is big for the small club.

* * *

Johnny Dundee doubled up last week, defeating K. O. Phil Delmont at the Broadway Club, Brooklyn on Monday and jumping over to Philadelphia Thanksgiving day when he defeated Alex Hart of Cleveland at the Ice Palace.

* * *

Two great matches are on tap for the Garden. On December 15, Ritchie Mitchell meets Charley White, while on the 22nd Joe Lynch will defend his title against Midget Smith.

* * *

Pepper Martin has started training for a busy campaign starting about the middle of December. He will box Kid Kaplan and will also box Jack Bernstein.

* * *

Jimmy Goodrich, the Buffalo junior lightweight who made a big hit in his initial start at the Garden, is booked for a bout with Billy DeFoe at Scranton and may also box Babe Herman at Toronto.

* * *

Charley Weinert lasted just three rounds against Gene Tunney at the Garden, November 29. Outclassed from the start, the Newark battler made a sorry showing and it looks as if he is through as far as the big clubs are concerned. The fight drew the smallest gate of the season at the big arena, less than $11,000 showing at the turnstiles.

* * *

Pancho Villa is the big noise among the flyweights and bantams right now. At Boston for defeating Young Montreal the little Filipino drew down over $6,000.

* * *

Hugh Hutchinson, Scotty Monteith's entry for featherweight honors, will shortly be seen in a Garden bout against a topnotcher. Hugh is a puncher and usually wins by a kayo. As the fans like a knockout artist Scotty should get plenty of engagements for his charge.

* * *

Jim Coffroth, the California sporting man, is again in our midst and it looks as if he is commuting between Frisco and New York. Probably Jim will become a permanent resident of the big town.

* * *

We have a little flyweight around the local clubs that is handicapped by the age ruling. Nat Pincus is his name and he wins consistently. In another year I predict that he will be boxing for the title.

* * *

### Heavyweight Hopes Do Their Stuff

New York—Gathered from the far jungles of fistiania, eighteen wild heavyweight critters were let loose in the old cage a week ago Monday night by James Johnston. Johnston was very eager to find among his strange assortment an opponent for Jack Dempsey.

Johnston at the close of the performance was still eager.

For several days the eighteen wild heavyweights, gathered from Wales, Texas, Harlem, Bushwick, California and Alabama, were kept in the circus zoo in the garden basement and fed on raw meat to make them wilder. Scouts from the beef trust entered the place by stealth shortly before the fights and almost ruined the show by kidnaping two of the rarest specimens, thereby mercifully abbreviating the show to nine bouts instead of ten.

The wildest critter exposed was a ferocious southpaw from the veldt of Bridgeport, Conn., responding to the name of Ed Keely. Mr. Keely, a 195 pounder, was sicked onto Frank Johns of Canarsie, also weighing 195 pounds. In the first round Mr. Keely cured Mr. Johns by hitting him a smack which pushed his face away. Mr. Johns was then lifted out to have his face restored to its wonted site.

Jimmy McMullen, who looked like an orphan, registered at the zoo from Dallas, Tex., was paired with Milton Goold, nee Goldfish, of Coney Island. McMullen wore a hair net and a pathetic expression. He did a magnificent swan dive in the third, but was wrapped around Milton's middle like an elastic girdle when the bout ended, the decision going to Milton.

Dan O'Connell of New Brunswick, N. J., known as the rector out of respect to the recent news from that choral center, won from Billy Smith, one of the Smiths of Newark, N. J., at 195 pounds. Mr. Smith expired in the fourth round.

There was some grumbling by the clientelle about short weight, two tons of heavyweights having been promised. When the total tonnage was counted it was discovered that slightly less than 3,500 pounds had been produced.

Mr. Dempsey's title is no less secure by reason of the evening's hilarity, than it was before the cages were opened.
